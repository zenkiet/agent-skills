## 1. Know your audience

- **You are not the user.** Design starts with who uses the product, their goals, tasks, vocabulary, skill and
  attitudes — not with screens.
- **Interactions are conversations:** a constant exchange of requests and responses. People need continuous
  feedback that input was received, work is progressing and results are ready.
- **Match the interaction style** to the audience: a short, tightly focused exchange vs. free exploration.
- **Skill level:** expert tools (Photoshop, Excel, IDEs, admin consoles), occasional-use tools (kiosks, checkout,
  installers, ATMs) and the large middle (email, presentations, social apps). Most products must teach newcomers
  *and* stay fast for experts: clear entry points and defaults for novices, shortcuts and power features for experts.
- **Goals:** finding a fact or object, learning, transacting, controlling/monitoring, creating, conversing, being
  entertained. The interface is only a means to these ends.
- **Ask why** (a few "whys", like root-cause analysis) to reach the real goal behind a feature request; solve the
  right problem, then solve it right. Account for softer factors: expectations, emotions, social context, values.

## 2. Research methods

- **Direct observation** (interviews, site visits): goals, tasks, environment, language.
- **Case studies:** deep views of a few representative or extreme users.
- **Surveys:** many respondents; careful question design needed.
- **Personas:** a synthesis tool for research data, not a data source.
Design research ≠ marketing research: you need behaviour and context, without biases such as self-selection.

## 3. Behavior patterns (design for how people actually act)

| Pattern | What people do | Design implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Safe Exploration** | Try things if mistakes are cheap | Undo, predictable Back, no surprise pop-ups or windows, reversible actions, visible system status |
| **Instant Gratification** | Want a success within seconds | Let people do something real immediately; delay sign-up and set-up questions; fast first screen |
| **Satisficing** | Pick the first plausible option | Clear calls to action, short scannable labels, layout that communicates meaning, easy recovery from wrong turns, low visual complexity |
| **Changes in Midstream** | Switch tasks halfway | Let people leave and come back; keep state and drafts; don't trap them in rigid flows |
| **Deferred Choices** | Skip non-essential questions | Few up-front choices, few required fields, good defaults, let people complete later from an obvious place |
| **Incremental Construction** | Build, review, revise in loops | Cheap edits, immediate preview, undo, partial saves |
| **Habituation** | Repeated actions become reflexes | Consistent gestures and shortcuts across the product; don't reuse a gesture for something dangerous; routine confirmation dialogs stop being read — use undo instead |
| **Microbreaks** | Use spare minutes | Fast launch, remembered sign-in, freshest content on the first screen |
| **Spatial Memory** | Find things by where they were | Keep tools, items and navigation in stable positions; don't reorder menus automatically |
| **Prospective Memory** | Leave reminders for later | Support flags, drafts, open windows, bookmarks, notes; don't auto-clean what users left on purpose |
| **Streamlined Repetition** | Repeat the same operation | Repeat-last-action, batch operations, find-and-replace, Macros |
| **Keyboard Only** | Keep hands on the keyboard | Shortcuts, logical tab order, arrow-key selection, Enter for the default action — vital for data entry and accessibility |
| **Social Proof & Collaboration** | Influenced by peers | Reviews, ratings, shared activity, co-editing and comments where relevant |

## 4. Beyond the screen (smart systems)

Products increasingly read context instead of waiting for input:
- **Connected devices** (phones, cars, thermostats) feed and are controlled by services.
- **Anticipatory systems** observe behaviour and suggest or act (reordering supplies) — be transparent and let
  people override.
- **Assistive systems** augment human capability.
- **Natural user interfaces** use gesture, touch, voice and motion.
The same principles apply: clear feedback, user control, recoverable actions, respect for privacy.
